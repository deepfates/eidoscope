---
title: "Why Stories Have Shapes"
date: "2025-06-08"
---
Across cultures, narratives recur in a handful of shapes: a protagonist wants something, obstacles escalate, a crisis forces change, a new equilibrium settles. These are not arbitrary conventions but tools for holding attention and encoding causal, social knowledge in memorable form. The specifics — the hero's journey, the tragic fall, the comedy of remarriage — are variations on managing tension and release. A story that violates the shape can feel profound or merely broken; the shape is the contract the audience did not know they signed.
