---
title: "Natural Selection as an Algorithm"
date: "2025-02-08"
---
Darwin's insight was almost embarrassingly simple: if traits vary, if variation is heritable, and if some variants reproduce more, then the population shifts toward those variants — no designer required. What looks like foresight is just the accumulated residue of differential survival. The subtlety is that selection is blind and local; it cannot plan, only ratchet. This is why evolution produces both breathtaking adaptation and absurd kludges like the recurrent laryngeal nerve. Understanding it as a substrate-independent algorithm — variation, selection, heredity — is what lets us see the same shape in immune systems, markets, and machine learning.
