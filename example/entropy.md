---
title: "Entropy and the Arrow of Time"
date: "2025-04-01"
---
The second law of thermodynamics says that in an isolated system entropy — roughly, the number of microscopic arrangements consistent with what we observe — tends to increase. This is why heat flows from hot to cold, why perpetual motion is impossible, and, strikingly, why time seems to have a direction at all. The microscopic laws are reversible; the asymmetry comes from starting in an improbably ordered state. Life is a local, temporary eddy of decreasing entropy paid for by exporting more disorder to its surroundings. Order is always borrowed.
