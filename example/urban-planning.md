---
title: "Cities Are Not Machines"
date: "2025-07-01"
---
Jane Jacobs' quarrel with mid-century planners was that they treated cities as machines to be rationalized — separate the functions, widen the roads, clear the messy old blocks. But a city's life comes from precisely the fine-grained mixing they destroyed: short blocks, mingled uses, eyes on the street, the slow accretion of local knowledge. A neighborhood is an emergent order no planner designs, only enables or wrecks. The recurring failure is imposing legible top-down structure on a system whose value lives in its illegible, bottom-up complexity.
