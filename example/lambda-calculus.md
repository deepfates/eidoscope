---
title: "The Lambda Calculus"
date: "2025-04-02"
---
With nothing but variables, function definition, and application, the lambda calculus is Turing-complete: it can express any computation. It is the theoretical seed of functional programming — Church's answer, contemporaneous with Turing's machine, to what "computable" means. Everything else (numbers, booleans, recursion, data structures) can be encoded as functions. The practical descendants are everywhere: closures, first-class functions, and the evaluation strategies (call-by-value, call-by-name) that language designers still argue about. It is a reminder that computation is not fundamentally about machines and memory, but about substitution and reduction.
