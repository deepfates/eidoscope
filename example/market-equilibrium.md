---
title: "Supply, Demand, and Prices as Signals"
date: "2025-03-05"
---
A price is a remarkably compressed piece of information: it aggregates the private knowledge and desires of everyone in a market into a single number that tells producers what to make and consumers what to forgo. When supply and demand balance, the market clears; when they do not, the price moves and behavior adjusts. Hayek's argument was epistemic, not moral — no central planner could gather and update the dispersed knowledge that prices encode automatically. The failures are real too: externalities, monopolies, and public goods are exactly the cases where prices lie.
