---
title: "Feedback Loops in the Climate System"
date: "2025-06-20"
---
The climate is dangerous less because of direct warming than because of its feedbacks. Melting ice exposes dark ocean that absorbs more heat, accelerating melting; warming releases methane from thawing permafrost, warming further. Some feedbacks are stabilizing — more warmth, more plant growth, more carbon uptake — but many amplify. This is why the system has tipping points, states from which return is slow or impossible. Modeling it means chasing coupled nonlinear loops where small pushes can trigger large, delayed, and sometimes irreversible responses.
