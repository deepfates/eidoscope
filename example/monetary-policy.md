---
title: "What Central Banks Actually Control"
date: "2025-05-28"
---
A central bank cannot dictate the economy; it nudges one variable — the short-term interest rate — and hopes the effects propagate through lending, spending, and expectations. Raise rates to cool inflation at the risk of unemployment; cut them to stimulate at the risk of overheating. Much of the real power is in expectations: if everyone believes the bank will keep inflation near target, the belief becomes partly self-fulfilling. The tools are blunt, the lags are long and variable, and the bank is always steering a car whose windshield shows only the past.
