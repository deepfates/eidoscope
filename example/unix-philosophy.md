---
title: "The Unix Philosophy"
date: "2025-06-14"
---
Write programs that do one thing well; make them speak a common interface (text streams); compose them with pipes rather than building monoliths. The Unix philosophy is a bet that small sharp tools, combined, beat large integrated ones — and that the composition surface should be dumb and universal. It has aged unevenly: the text-stream substrate is lossy, and structured data keeps wanting richer pipes. But the core instinct — favor composition over configuration, keep each piece replaceable — remains the most reliable guide to software that stays malleable as it grows.
